General Conference Study Companion

Individual Project Site Plan

Site Name

General Conference Study Companion

Project name was chosen because the website will serve as a companion for people studying General Conference messages. It communicates that the site will help users understand, remember, compare and apply teachings from living prophets and Church leaders.

The word companion reflects the goal of providing a helpful resource that users can return to during personal study, family sudy, lesson preparation, or times when they need spiritual guidance.

Possible domain name: conference-study-companion.org

Site Purpose

The purpose of the General Conference Study Companion is to help users retain and apply important teachings from General Conference. The website will present concise summaries, key principles, supporting scriptures, memorable statements, and modern-day applications from selected talks.

Instead of requiring users to reread long talks every time they want to remember a message, the site will organize the main teachings into shorter and easier-to-review sections.

The completed website will help users

  • Review concise summaries of selected General Conference talks.
  • Connect prophetic teachings with related scriptures.
  • Consider how each teaching applies to modern-day challenges.
  • Compare messages that teach related gospel principles.
  • Save favorite talks and mark talks as studied.
  • Submit a request for a talk or topic to be added to the study collection.

Target Audience

The target audience includes members of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints and others who want to understand the teachings shared during General Conference.

The site may be especially helpful for individuals preparing talks or lessons, youth studying the words of living prophets, families discussing conference messages, and people who want to remember and apply conference teachings more consistently.

Scenarios

The following questions represent needs that members of the target audience may have:

  • What were the main principles taught in a General Conference talk without rereading the entire message?
  • Which General Conference talks address a challenge such as anxiety, forgiveness, faith, prayer, or finding peace?
  • How can I apply teachings from this conference talk to a modern-day situation in my own life?
  • Which scriptures support the central message of this talk?
  • How does one General Conference message compare with another message about the same principle?
  • How can I keep track of the talks I have studied and save the messages I want to review again?

The color scheme uses peaceful, diginified colors that support the spiritual and educational purpose of the website.

Typography

Merriweather

Merriweather will be used for the website name, page headings, section headings, and important titles. Its traditional appearance supports the respectful and thoughtful purpose of the website.

Lato

Lato will be used for paragraphs, navigation links, buttons, labels, lists and other general content. Its clean letter shapes make longer sections of information easy to read on both small and large screens.

Planned Website Content

The completed project will contain at least 15 study items. Each study item will represent a selected General Conference talk or message.

Each talk may include:

  • Speaker name
  • Talk title
  • Conference date
  • Short summary
  • Main gospel principle
  • Related scriptures
  • Modern-day application
  • Questions to ponder
  • A more detailed modal window
  • A favorite or studied option

Planned Interactivity

JavaScript will be used to load and display the General Conference study items dynamically.

The planned interactive features include:

  • Dynamically generated talk cards created from JavaScript data.
  • A modal dialog that displays additional information about a selected talk.
  • Buttons that allow users to mark talks as favorites.
  • A studied-status feature that helps users monitor their study progress.
  • Local storage that remembers favorite and studied choices after the browser is closed
  • A form that allows users to request another conference talk or gospel topic.

Home Page Wireframe

The wireframes show the planned structure of the home page on a mobile screen and on a wider desktop or tablet screen. The sketches focus on page organization rather than colors, images, fonts, or detailed visual design.
